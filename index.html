01 The observability gap

The ocean is connected.
But it's not yet observable.

A rapidly growing ecosystem of underwater sensors, autonomous vehicles, smart buoys, satellites, and AI systems is creating the Internet of Underwater Things (IoUT): a digital system for the ocean.

Yet a critical gap remains. Current ocean monitoring systems measure environmental conditions — temperature, salinity, pH, dissolved oxygen, water quality. They rarely measure the biological and geochemical activity that determines ecosystem health, carbon cycling, infrastructure integrity, and operational risk.

This gap affects far more than carbon markets. Offshore energy assets degrade without warning. Ports struggle to meet environmental compliance. Aquaculture operations lose stock to unseen water quality shifts. Subsea cables and pipelines lack persistent awareness of their surrounding environment. Across all five markets, operators and regulators operate on inference rather than evidence.

Environmental state ≠ function or risk

Knowing temperature and pH doesn't tell you whether carbon is being transformed, whether a foundation is at risk of corrosion, or whether port conditions are safe. Biological and geochemical activity — the actual processes — remain inferred, not measured.

Sparse samples, high stakes

A handful of discrete water samples per quarter cannot resolve carbon removal performance, detect pipeline corrosion onset, or catch pollution events in a port. Across every vertical, sparse data means delayed decisions and accumulated risk.

Costly monitoring, partial picture

Today's approach — bespoke sensors, discrete sampling, separate systems for each vertical — delivers narrow, incomparable data at high cost. Operators pay for a slice of the picture in carbon, energy, ports, aquaculture, and defense alike.

02 The observability platform

From periodic sampling
to continuous intelligence.

Enthalpic is not fundamentally selling sensors. We sell continuous ocean intelligence — the ability to transform underwater environments from periodically sampled systems into continuously monitored assets.

Our platform combines thermodynamic sensing with AI-powered analytics to measure biological and geochemical activity directly. The application changes — from carbon credits to offshore energy to defense — but the core capability remains the same: real-time understanding of ecosystem function and infrastructure health through advanced sensing and intelligence.

The same core technology serves marine carbon removal today, and offshore energy, ports, aquaculture, and maritime infrastructure tomorrow.

05 How it works

Next-generation sensing
for ecosystem observability.

Quantum-enabled biogeochemical sensing

Our core sensor technology uses quantum-sensitive materials to detect minute biogeochemical signatures from carbon transformations and biological activity. Developed in partnership with Fraunhofer Institute (Start-a-Factory, Berlin). Provides real-time, in-situ observation of ecosystem processes at sensitivities conventional systems cannot reach.

Microfluidic sampling

Low-power, in-situ microfluidic platforms for automated collection and analysis of porewater and seawater samples. Enables high-resolution biogeochemical profiling across depth and time for carbon cycling, nutrient dynamics, and ecosystem health assessment.

AI ecosystem intelligence

Probabilistic models that fuse sensor data with physical priors to quantify ecosystem function, carbon permanence, and transformation pathway attribution. Pathway-agnostic by design — applies equally to OAE, biomass, blue carbon, aquaculture, and natural ecosystem monitoring.

Quantum sensor and microfluidic technology developed in partnership with Fraunhofer Institute (Start-a-Factory, Berlin). AI ecosystem intelligence is in early prototyping.

07 Why now

Five markets converging
on the same need.

Advances in marine sensors, autonomous systems, edge computing, satellite connectivity, and artificial intelligence are generating unprecedented volumes of ocean data. The Internet of Underwater Things is becoming reality — creating the infrastructure for a connected, observable ocean.

Yet most systems monitor environmental state rather than biological and geochemical activity. Carbon markets, offshore energy operators, ports, aquaculture farms, and defense agencies all face the same bottleneck: they need continuous intelligence about what is happening in the water, not just what is present.

At the same time, protection of critical underwater infrastructure has become a national security priority for the EU, NATO, and the United States. The same sensing and intelligence platform that serves carbon markets also serves this urgent need — making Enthalpic more resilient, more defensible, and more timely than a single-vertical approach.

08 Status

Built at Carbon 13,
based at LEAP Berlin.

Enthalpic was founded by Alberto Robador, an assistant professor for geomicrobiology and environmental biogeochemistry at the University of Southern California (Los Angeles, US), whose research sits at the intersection of microbial energetics, thermodynamics, and environmental sensing.

Alberto is part of the Carbon 13 venture builder program and is developing Enthalpic from LEAP Berlin, within Berlin's Quantum Technology Hub, with ongoing conversations with mCDR project operators and developers, marine biogeochemistry research groups, and ecosystem monitoring partners.

The company is at an early stage, currently focused on sensor prototyping for biological and chemical carbon transformation signals, AI model development, and partnership development with field-deployment and research partners.
